# Product Discovery, Roadmap Strategy, and Technical Product Development

## Context

GAUSS is a statistical programming and data analysis platform used by economists, researchers, and analysts. Its development team was lean, so every release involved hard choices about what to build, what to change, and what to leave for later. Those choices were only as good as the team's understanding of how customers actually worked.

At Aptech Systems, I was a consistent source of that understanding. My title was Director of Training and Applications, but product development and product strategy were a core part of the role: I participated in customer discovery, annual release planning, feature prioritization, API and UX evaluation, technical testing, and GTM decisions.

## Customer Discovery: Expanding the GAUSS Time Series Library

### The question

The Time Series Library served a core group of GAUSS users. The team needed to decide how the library should evolve, and with limited development capacity, which improvements would matter most to the people who relied on it.

### What I did

- **Helped design the customer survey** used to gather broad input on needs and priorities.
- **Reached out to roughly 200 to 300 users** to invite participation.
- **Took part in roughly 10 to 15 in-depth interviews** with core users to understand their workflows in detail.
- **Identified customer needs and workflow limitations:** where the library fell short of what users were trying to do, and where they were working around it.
- **Translated feedback into product requirements and roadmap priorities** the development team could act on.
- **Helped prioritize features** to fit the capacity of a lean development team.

### What changed

Customer input directly shaped the roadmap. One important result was an expansion of the library's **structural VAR capabilities** and broader implementation options, based on what core users said they needed. The features that came out of this work are described in [Time-Series Product Development and API Design in GAUSS](time-series-gauss.md).

**The pattern:** customer research → qualitative insight → product requirements → roadmap decision.

## Annual Release Planning: Representing the Customer in Product Decisions

Each year, I participated in feature review and planning for the annual GAUSS release. My contribution was to bring customer and user needs into decisions that could otherwise be driven only by technical considerations.

### What I did

- Reviewed proposed features against what customers were asking for and struggling with.
- Represented customer and user needs in product discussions.
- Helped prioritize features for a lean development team.
- Contributed to API development.
- Conducted product QA, technical testing, and UX evaluation.
- Evaluated whether proposed capabilities would be understandable and useful to end users, not just technically correct.
- Participated in GTM positioning decisions for new capabilities.

Through this process, features were routinely prioritized, changed, or deprioritized based on technical constraints, customer needs, and product priorities.

## How I Evaluate Technical Products

The same questions run through my work on discovery, release planning, the [GAUSS Machine Learning Library](product-development-launch.md), and [TSPDLIB](tspdlib-library.md):

- **Use case:** Who is this for, and what are they trying to accomplish?
- **API and syntax:** Is the interface consistent with the rest of the product? Can a user predict how it works?
- **Functionality:** Does it work correctly across realistic inputs and edge cases?
- **Output and interpretability:** Can users read the results and understand what they mean without consulting a manual?
- **Adoption:** What documentation, examples, and positioning will users need to get value from it?
